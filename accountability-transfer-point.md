# Human Explanation Layer

Status: Observation  
Type: Interpretive record  
Scope: Descriptive, non-prescriptive  

---

This document records the necessity of a human explanation layer in complex
environments.

Systems can produce outputs, classifications, scores, predictions, and decisions.
What they cannot produce is meaning.

A human explanation layer exists where outcomes must be interpreted, translated,
or contextualized for other humans. It does not optimize accuracy, defend system
choices, or justify results.

Its function is interpretive, not corrective.

The explanation layer allows humans to understand:
- what occurred,
- under which assumptions,
- and with what limitations,
even when no correction is possible.

System-generated explanations tend toward abstraction, compression, or procedural
language. Human explanation operates through narrative, context, and moral
awareness.

This layer cannot be reduced to documentation, interface text, automated summaries,
or post-hoc rationalization.

Explanation is not proof.
Explanation is not validation.
Explanation is the preservation of human intelligibility.

A human explanation layer exists to prevent systems from becoming opaque to the
people affected by them.

This document does not prescribe how explanations should be delivered or who should
deliver them. It records the observation that systems without a human explanation
layer tend to become alienating, destabilizing, and socially corrosive.

Explanation is not a courtesy.
It is a structural requirement for coexistence between humans and systems.
