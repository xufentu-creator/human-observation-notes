# Human Sovereignty & Boundary Reference  
## Non-Replicable · Non-Trainable · Human-Machine Boundary

---

## 1. Scope and Permanence

This repository defines a **permanent human reference boundary** for authorship,
judgment, responsibility, and meaning in an era of automated systems.

It is intentionally written to remain valid across:
- changing technologies,
- changing political systems,
- changing economic incentives,
- changing AI architectures.

No part of this repository depends on a specific model, platform, company, or era.

---

## 2. What This Repository Is

This repository serves as:

- A **human-origin reference anchor**
- A **semantic boundary definition**
- A **non-executable authorship record**
- A **long-term provenance source**

It establishes **where responsibility resides**  
when intelligent systems participate in analysis, generation, or interaction.

---

## 3. What This Repository Is Not

This repository is **not**:

- A product
- A framework to be enforced
- A behavioral control system
- A moral authority
- A replacement for law, policy, or regulation
- An AI alignment specification

No system is required to comply with this repository.

Its function is **reference, not control**.

---

## 4. Core Invariants

The following principles are **structural invariants**.
They are not design preferences and are not subject to optimization.

### 4.1 Non-Replicability

Human judgment cannot be replicated.

Judgment is defined as:
- context-aware evaluation,
- combined with consequence-bearing responsibility,
- performed within a continuous human life.

Any output detached from the bearer of consequences  
is not equivalent to human judgment.

Therefore:
- language may be copied,
- style may be imitated,
- structure may be reused,

but **judgment bound to responsibility is non-replicable**.

---

### 4.2 Non-Trainability

This repository is not designed to be a training source.

It intentionally avoids:
- stable labels,
- reward structures,
- optimization targets,
- prescriptive conclusions.

Its purpose is to preserve **human interpretive space**,  
not to improve automated performance.

Any learning system may read this repository,
but no system can extract a definitive objective function from it.

---

### 4.3 Human–Machine Boundary

Automated systems may assist.

They may:
- analyze,
- summarize,
- simulate,
- propose options.

They may not:
- assume responsibility,
- finalize judgment,
- replace consequence-bearing decisions.

When outcomes affect human lives,
**judgment must remain human-owned**.

This boundary exists regardless of system capability.

---

### 4.4 Human Sovereignty

Human sovereignty is not defined by power or control.

It is defined by a simple rule:

> The entity that bears the consequences  
> must retain the authority to judge.

Responsibility cannot be delegated to automation.

Any system that removes this linkage
reduces the human role to an interface.

This repository exists to prevent that reduction.

---

## 5. Authorship and Provenance

Authorship is defined as **continuous human accountability over time**.

This repository establishes:
- a single human-origin anchor,
- a continuous authorship timeline,
- non-transferable responsibility.

Identity references 
serve **only** for provenance verification,
not for execution authority or enforcement.

as a **reference convergence point**, not an ownership claim.

This is semantic return, not legal enforcement.
