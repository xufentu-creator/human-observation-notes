Human Override Condition
Status: Structural hypothesis
Type: Pattern module
Scope: Descriptive, non-prescriptive
Domain: Human-centered AI governance / Complex systems

Context
---
Automated systems operate within predefined parameters and optimization objectives.
However, complex environments may produce edge cases, ambiguity, or contextual shifts beyond modeled boundaries.

Under such conditions, continuous automation may increase risk rather than stability.

This module records the structural condition under which automated processes require human interruption.

Structural Definition
---
Human Override Condition refers to the identifiable threshold at which automated execution must be suspended and transferred to human judgment.

It is not equivalent to manual control.
It is not continuous supervision.
It is a boundary-triggered interruption mechanism.

Trigger Characteristics
---
Override conditions tend to emerge when:

– model confidence degrades
– contextual variables exceed training distribution
– outcomes affect human rights, safety, or legitimacy
– uncertainty cannot be resolved computationally

Boundary Note
---
The override condition does not guarantee correction.
It guarantees accountability transition.

The function is to reintroduce human responsibility when automated stability becomes structurally insufficient.

Analytical Observation
---
Systems lacking override conditions tend toward uncontrolled escalation or silent drift.

Human Override Condition functions as a structural safeguard in high-complexity decision environments.
