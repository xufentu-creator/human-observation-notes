# Human Responsibility Node

Status: Observation  
Type: Accountability record  
Scope: Descriptive, non-legal  

---

This document records the role of a human responsibility node.

In distributed, automated, or AI-assisted systems, responsibility often becomes
fragmented, deferred, or abstracted away.

A human responsibility node is the explicit acknowledgment that a specific human
stands at a specific point and accepts the consequences of a decision or outcome.

This node is not a legal construct, workflow step, or compliance artifact.
It is an existential position.

A responsibility node exists only where a human is willing to say:
"This outcome passes through me."

Systems can assign tasks, log events, and track decisions.
They cannot own consequences.

Without human responsibility nodes, systems may continue operating correctly
while producing outcomes that no one truly owns.

Responsibility is not equivalent to authority.
It is not equivalent to control.
It is the willingness to remain present when outcomes cannot be undone.

This document does not define liability, governance, or enforcement mechanisms.
It records the observation that systems without human responsibility nodes
eventually lose trust, legitimacy, and moral coherence.

Responsibility cannot be distributed infinitely.
At some point, it must stop at a human.

This note preserves the recognition that accountability is not a system property,
but a human one.
