Human Explanation Layer
---
Status: Structural hypothesis
Type: Pattern module
Scope: Descriptive, non-prescriptive
Domain: Human-centered AI governance / Complex systems

Context

In AI-mediated environments, automated systems generate outputs that influence human perception, evaluation, and decision-making. These outputs may include classifications, risk scores, recommendations, predictions, or procedural actions.

As system complexity increases, the interpretability gap between system output and human understanding may widen. When system outputs affect human outcomes, intelligibility becomes structurally relevant. This document records the structural observation that an interpretive mediation layer tends to emerge or become necessary under such conditions.

Structural Definition

The Human Explanation Layer refers to an interpretive mediation function positioned between system output and human intelligibility.

It does not modify computational processes. It does not alter model parameters. It does not validate system correctness. Its function is interpretive rather than corrective.

The Human Explanation Layer translates system outputs into contextualized meaning frameworks accessible to affected human actors.

Functional Characteristics

The Human Explanation Layer operates through contextualization, assumption articulation, limitation disclosure, and human-oriented framing.

Contextualization situates outputs within environmental, institutional, or situational frames. Assumption articulation clarifies implicit model constraints and operational boundaries. Limitation disclosure identifies uncertainty ranges and scope limits. Human-oriented framing aligns interpretation with social, ethical, or experiential reference points.

These characteristics support intelligibility rather than optimization.

Boundary Conditions

The relevance of the Human Explanation Layer increases when system outputs directly affect individuals or groups, when automated processes lack immediate transparency, when interpretability influences trust or legitimacy, and when decision environments involve uncertainty or high stakes.

The layer may be structurally unnecessary in purely mechanical contexts where interpretability does not influence human stability, coordination, or accountability.

Failure Pattern

In the absence of an interpretive mediation layer, system outputs may become opaque to affected individuals.

Opacity does not imply computational error. It indicates a structural disconnect between automated output and human semantic frameworks.

Sustained opacity may correlate with erosion of trust, interpretive conflict, perceived alienation, and instability in human-system coordination. These outcomes are contingent rather than inevitable, but tend to increase in environments of high uncertainty.

Conceptual Distinction

The Human Explanation Layer is not equivalent to documentation, interface text, automated summary generation, post-hoc justification, or model validation.

It refers specifically to an interpretive mediation function that preserves intelligibility between automated systems and human actors.

Analytical Note

This module does not prescribe implementation models, governance mechanisms, or institutional responsibility allocation.

It records the structural hypothesis that interpretive mediation functions as a stabilizing interface in complex, AI-driven environments.

The Human Explanation Layer is defined here as a structural pattern rather than a normative mandate.
