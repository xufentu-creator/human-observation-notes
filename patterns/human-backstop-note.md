# Human Backstop

Status: Observation  
Type: Human judgment record  
Scope: Descriptive, non-executable  

---

This document records a recurring condition observed across complex, automated,
or optimization-driven systems.

When systems fail, stall, or produce outcomes that cannot be resolved within
their own logic, a human backstop becomes necessary.

A human backstop is not a feature, escalation path, support function, or control
mechanism. It is the presence of a human who can interrupt execution, suspend
optimization, and absorb uncertainty that the system itself cannot carry.

Systems are designed to proceed.
Humans are capable of stopping.

This stopping capacity is not inefficiency. It is a safety condition.

The backstop exists precisely because some situations do not have a correct,
optimal, or computable answer. They require a human to pause, reconsider context,
and accept that uncertainty itself is part of reality.

A human backstop cannot be automated, delegated, or simulated. It exists only
where a human is willing to intervene without guarantees.

This document does not define triggers, workflows, or escalation rules.
It records the observation that systems without a human backstop tend to:
- fail silently,
- defer responsibility,
- externalize harm,
- or continue operating despite unresolved consequences.

The absence of a human backstop does not make systems faster or smarter.
It makes them brittle.

This note preserves the recognition that human interruption is not a flaw in
system design, but a requirement for systems to remain compatible with reality.
