Decision Interface Boundary
---
Status: Structural hypothesis
Type: Pattern module

Scope: Descriptive, non-prescriptive
Domain: Human-centered AI governance / Complex systems

Context
---
In AI-mediated environments, automated systems increasingly generate recommendations, classifications, scores, triggers, or executable outputs that influence human decisions.

As automation penetrates decision environments, a structural question emerges:

Where does automated processing end and human judgment begin?

The Decision Interface Boundary refers to the structural threshold at which system-generated output transitions into human responsibility-bearing action.

This boundary becomes relevant when system outputs affect outcomes that carry social, ethical, legal, or strategic consequences.

Structural Definition
---
The Decision Interface Boundary is the structural demarcation between:

System computation
and
Human decision adoption

It does not describe technical architecture alone.
It describes the moment at which a human actor:

Accepts
Modifies
Overrides
or Rejects

a system-generated output.

The boundary is therefore not computational but responsibility-oriented.

Functional Characteristics
---
The Decision Interface Boundary exhibits several structural properties:

Visibility — The transition from system output to human adoption is identifiable rather than implicit.

Interruptibility — Human actors retain the capacity to pause or override automated recommendations.

Accountability Localization — Responsibility for final decision outcomes is assignable to human actors beyond the computational layer.

Cognitive Awareness — Human actors understand that a boundary exists and recognize their role within it.

These characteristics contribute to structural clarity in AI-mediated environments.

Boundary Conditions
---
The structural relevance of the Decision Interface Boundary increases when:

System outputs influence high-stakes decisions.

Automation compresses decision timeframes.

Outputs are probabilistic rather than deterministic.

Institutional responsibility must be preserved.

In low-risk or purely mechanical contexts, the boundary may be less visible, though it still structurally exists.

Failure Pattern
---
When the Decision Interface Boundary becomes blurred or implicit, several structural risks may emerge:

Automation Drift — Human actors defer to system output without reflective engagement.

Responsibility Diffusion — Accountability becomes ambiguous between system and operator.

Cognitive Passivity — Humans transition from decision-makers to output executors.

Legitimacy Erosion — Affected individuals cannot identify who holds final authority.

These risks do not arise from computational failure, but from structural ambiguity at the boundary.

Conceptual Distinction
---
The Decision Interface Boundary is not:

A user interface element
A software permission setting
A workflow step
A compliance checkbox

It refers to the structural threshold of decision authority transfer.

It is distinct from the Human Explanation Layer.

The Explanation Layer concerns interpretive intelligibility.
The Decision Interface Boundary concerns authority localization.

Analytical Note
---
This module does not prescribe governance mechanisms, regulatory standards, or implementation procedures.

It records the structural observation that sustainable human–AI coexistence requires an identifiable boundary at which decision authority transitions from system computation to human responsibility.

The Decision Interface Boundary is defined here as a structural pattern rather than a normative mandate.
