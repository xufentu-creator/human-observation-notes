# Accountability Transfer Point

Status: Structural hypothesis
Type: Pattern module
Scope: Descriptive, non-prescriptive
Domain: Human-centered AI governance / Complex systems

Context
---
In AI-mediated systems, actions may originate from automated computation but produce human-impacting outcomes.

Responsibility attribution becomes structurally ambiguous when execution and consequence are distributed across human and machine agents.

This module records the structural point at which accountability shifts.

Structural Definition
---
Accountability Transfer Point refers to the identifiable moment or threshold at which responsibility transitions from automated execution to human ownership.

It does not determine fault.
It identifies structural custody of consequence.

Functional Characteristics
---

Transfer points often occur when:

automated outputs are acted upon  
institutional authority validates system recommendations  
human actors endorse or implement machine outputs  
irreversible consequences become probable

Boundary Clarification
---
The transfer point does not imply system failure.
It clarifies responsibility location within complex interaction networks.

Analytical Observation
---
Without clearly identifiable accountability transfer points, responsibility diffusion tends to occur.

Diffusion increases systemic instability, legal ambiguity, and ethical erosion.

Accountability Transfer Points function as structural anchors for governance clarity.
