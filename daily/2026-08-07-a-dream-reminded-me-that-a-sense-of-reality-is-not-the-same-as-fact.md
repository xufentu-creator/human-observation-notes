# 2026年8月7日｜一个梦提醒我：真实感不等于事实

**作者：** Xufen Tu
**记录类型：** Public Observation Record
**主题：** Human Judgment · Trust · Perception

---

今天坐在那里睡着了一会儿，做了一个很清楚的梦。梦里，一个以前认识的人坐在沙发上和我聊天。我看见他好像变了，头发没有了。我们像平常一样说话，气氛一开始并不特别可怕，但我能感觉到他和过去不完全一样。聊着聊着，他突然说，你看我的眼睛变小了，耳朵也变小了。我在梦里很努力地睁开眼睛，确实看到他的眼睛很小很小，耳朵也很小很小。可是很奇怪，我并没有立刻停下来认真看，像是看见了，又没有真正看进去，很快又恢复成正常聊天的样子。

后来他变得有点着急，好像在提醒我：你看不见吗？我的眼睛，我的耳朵。那一刻我在梦里觉得自己的眼睛很难睁开，像被什么东西压住一样。我使劲睁开，终于看到他的耳朵上好像挂着一根针，像是穿过去的。那个画面很清楚，也很突然。然后我继续努力睁眼，就醒来了。

醒来以后，这个梦给我的感觉很强。它不像普通很快忘掉的梦，而是留下了一种“我是不是没有看清什么”的感觉。人的第一反应很容易把这样的梦往外解释：是不是对方在告诉我什么，是不是某种灵魂之间的联系，是不是上天安排了什么需要我执行的事情。但我现在更谨慎地看待这种感受。梦的真实感很强，并不等于它就是现实信息，也不等于它可以变成行动命令。

我更愿意把它先放回人的感知和判断里理解。最近我一直在想关系、信任、翻脸、真实与看见这些问题。一个人突然变化，一个熟悉的人变得陌生，一个人在梦里反复说“你看不见吗”，这些画面可能不是在传达外部指令，而是在把我最近的感受用梦的方式呈现出来。眼睛变小，像是看不清；耳朵变小，像是听不见；耳朵被针穿过，像是听见某些事情本身带着痛感。梦里的“看”和“听”，其实都和判断有关。

这让我想到，人在现实中也常常处在类似状态里。很多事情不是没有出现，而是出现的时候我们没有完全看清。一个人的变化、关系里的细小失衡、语气里的转向、信任里的裂缝，很多时候一开始都不是特别明显。人会因为过去的熟悉感继续和对方说话，会因为不愿意把事情想坏而忽略不舒服的地方，也会因为还想维持关系而不去认真看某些变化。等到对方很急地说“你看不见吗”，其实可能是心里某一部分在提醒自己：有些东西已经变了，只是你还没有真正承认。

但这仍然不代表梦本身就是事实。

梦可以提醒人注意自己的感受，却不能替人确认现实。梦里一个人变了，不代表现实中的那个人一定发生了什么；梦里一个画面很强，不代表它具有外部命令；梦里出现痛苦和提醒，也不代表醒来以后就应该联系对方、指责对方、改变关系或执行什么行动。梦更像一面很不稳定的镜子，它可能照见情绪，也可能放大恐惧，可能组合记忆，也可能把最近的思考变成画面。它值得记录，但不能直接相信。

这正是我今天觉得重要的地方：人的感知并不总是等于事实。真实感越强，人越容易把它当成真实。一个梦很清楚，一段情绪很强，一个画面很震撼，都会让人觉得“这一定有意义”。可是有意义不等于有命令，有感受不等于有事实，有提醒不等于有结论。人需要在感受和行动之间停一下，把梦、情绪、记忆和现实重新分开。

这也和我一直关注的人类判断有关。判断不是否定感受，而是不让感受直接变成执行。一个人醒来以后，可以承认这个梦让自己不安，也可以承认它触碰到了关系里的某些担心，但不能因为它很像真的，就把它当作对现实的确认。真正负责任的做法，是先记录，再观察，再回到现实中看有没有具体事实，而不是把梦里的画面直接变成对他人的判断。

今天这个梦也让我想到，很多人会在不确定时寻找一种外部解释。因为如果这是某种安排，就好像自己只需要执行；如果这是某种信息，就好像现实已经替自己确认；如果这是某种命令，就好像判断的压力可以被交出去。但人的难处就在这里：很多时候，没有谁替我们完成确认。梦不能替我们确认，情绪不能替我们确认，强烈的真实感也不能替我们确认。最终，人还是要醒来以后重新看现实，重新分辨什么只是感受，什么有事实支持，什么可以继续观察，什么不能进入行动。

所以我今天不把这个梦理解成灵魂通话，也不把它理解成上天安排的执行命令。我会把它理解成一次关于“看见、听见和判断”的提醒。它提醒我，有些关系里的变化需要看见，但看见不能急着变成结论；有些感受很强烈，但强烈不能替代事实；有些画面让人不安，但不安本身不是证据。

梦可以被记录，但不能被直接执行。真实感很强，不等于事实已经成立。越是强烈的感受，越需要醒来后的判断。

今天留下的问题是：当一个梦、一个画面或一种强烈感受让人觉得“很真实”时，人应该怎样既不压掉自己的感受，也不让感受越过事实，直接变成判断和行动？

# August 7, 2026 | A Dream Reminded Me That a Sense of Reality Is Not the Same as Fact

Today I fell asleep for a short while while sitting there, and I had a very clear dream. In the dream, someone I used to know was sitting on a sofa and talking with me. I saw that he seemed to have changed. His hair was gone. We were speaking almost normally, and at first the atmosphere did not feel especially frightening, but I could sense that he was not exactly the same as before. As we continued talking, he suddenly said, “Look, my eyes have become smaller, and my ears have become smaller too.” In the dream, I tried very hard to open my eyes, and I did see that his eyes were very, very small, and his ears were also very small. But strangely, I did not immediately stop and look carefully. It was as if I had seen it, but had not really taken it in, and then the conversation returned to normal.

Later, he became a little anxious, as if he was reminding me: “Can’t you see it? My eyes, my ears.” At that moment, in the dream, I felt that my eyes were very difficult to open, as if something was pressing them down. I forced them open, and then I saw that there seemed to be a needle hanging through his ear, as if it had pierced through it. The image was very clear and very sudden. Then I kept trying to open my eyes, and I woke up.

After waking, the dream left a strong feeling. It was not like an ordinary dream that disappears quickly. It left behind the feeling of “was there something I failed to see clearly?” A person’s first reaction may be to interpret a dream like this externally: is the other person trying to tell me something, is this some kind of connection between souls, is this something arranged by God that I must execute? But I now look at this feeling more carefully. A dream can feel very real, but that does not mean it is real-world information, and it does not mean it can become a command for action.

I would rather first place it back within perception and judgment. Recently, I have been thinking about relationships, trust, people turning away, reality, and seeing clearly. A person suddenly changing, a familiar person becoming unfamiliar, someone repeatedly saying in a dream, “Can’t you see it?”—these images may not be delivering an external instruction. They may be presenting recent feelings in the form of a dream. Smaller eyes feel like not being able to see clearly. Smaller ears feel like not being able to hear. A needle piercing through an ear feels like the pain of hearing something. In the dream, “seeing” and “hearing” were both connected to judgment.

This made me think that in real life, people are often in a similar state. Many things do not fail to appear. They appear, but we do not fully see them at the beginning. Changes in a person, small imbalances in a relationship, shifts in tone, and cracks in trust often do not look obvious at first. People continue speaking with someone because of past familiarity. They ignore discomfort because they do not want to think badly. They avoid looking carefully because they still want to preserve the relationship. By the time someone urgently says, “Can’t you see it?” it may actually be one part of the self reminding another part: something has changed, but you have not truly admitted it yet.

But this still does not mean the dream itself is fact.

A dream can remind a person to notice their feelings, but it cannot confirm reality on their behalf. A person changing in a dream does not mean that the person in real life has necessarily changed in that way. A strong image in a dream does not mean it carries an external command. Pain and warning inside a dream do not mean that after waking, one should contact someone, accuse someone, change a relationship, or carry out any action. A dream is more like an unstable mirror. It may reflect emotion, amplify fear, combine memory, or turn recent thoughts into images. It is worth recording, but it should not be directly believed.

This is what felt important to me today: human perception is not always the same as fact. The stronger the sense of reality, the easier it is to mistake it for reality itself. A clear dream, a strong emotion, or a shocking image can all make a person feel, “This must mean something.” But meaning is not the same as command. Feeling is not the same as fact. A reminder is not the same as conclusion. A person needs to pause between feeling and action, and separate dream, emotion, memory, and reality again.

This also connects to my ongoing concern with human judgment. Judgment does not mean denying feeling. It means not allowing feeling to move directly into execution. After waking, a person can acknowledge that the dream was unsettling, and can also acknowledge that it touched certain worries about relationships. But just because it felt real does not mean it should be treated as confirmation of reality. The more responsible action is to record it, observe it, and return to reality to see whether there are concrete facts, rather than turning the dream image directly into a judgment about another person.

Today’s dream also made me think about how people often look for an external explanation when they are uncertain. If something is an arrangement, then it may feel like one only needs to execute it. If something is a message, then it may feel like reality has already confirmed it. If something is a command, then it may feel like the burden of judgment can be handed away. But the difficulty of being human is exactly here: very often, nothing confirms things on our behalf. Dreams cannot confirm for us. Emotions cannot confirm for us. A strong sense of reality cannot confirm for us. In the end, after waking, human beings still need to look again at reality, distinguish what is only feeling, what is supported by fact, what can continue to be observed, and what must not enter action.

So today, I do not understand this dream as a soul conversation, nor as a command arranged by God. I understand it as a reminder about seeing, hearing, and judgment. It reminds me that some changes in relationships may need to be seen, but seeing should not rush into conclusion. Some feelings are strong, but strength cannot replace fact. Some images are unsettling, but unease itself is not evidence.

A dream can be recorded, but it should not be directly executed. A strong sense of reality does not mean that fact has been established. The stronger the feeling, the more judgment is needed after waking.

The question I am left with today is this: when a dream, an image, or a strong feeling makes something feel “very real,” how can a person avoid suppressing the feeling, while also preventing the feeling from crossing over facts and directly becoming judgment and action?
