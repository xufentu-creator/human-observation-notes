# 2026-07-14｜研究的目的：让知识进入真实世界

我持续研究人工智能治理、复杂系统、人类判断与决策结构，并不是为了建立更多概念，也不是为了追逐某一种短期技术趋势。

真正让我长期投入研究的，是一个越来越现实的问题：当技术不断进入人类生活以后，人们怎样仍然能够理解自己正在面对什么，怎样保留判断，怎样避免责任在复杂流程中被无意转移，以及怎样让技术真正帮助人，而不是让人越来越难理解自己参与的系统。

我目前继续在美国学习与开展独立研究。这里让我能够持续接触不同的社会环境、技术实践、商业场景与现实问题，也不断促使我重新思考人与技术之间的关系。

我的研究目标并不是停留在理论表达，而是希望这些长期积累能够逐渐转化为现实价值，帮助社区、企业、组织和普通人在人工智能时代作出更清楚的判断，更安全地使用技术，并让新的系统始终保留必要的人类确认、责任边界与现实判断。

我始终关注那些经常被技术讨论忽略的人。

他们可能是小企业经营者、普通劳动者、技术能力有限的人、需要重新学习新工具的人，也可能是在快速变化的系统中承担真实责任，却缺乏足够资源表达自己的人。

技术的发展不应该只服务于已经拥有最多资源的人。

真正有价值的技术，也应该帮助普通人降低理解门槛，改善工作方式，获得新的机会，并在不断变化的环境中保留尊严、选择与主动性。

我的研究首先关注美国社会正在面对的现实问题，希望能够通过长期实践，为社区、企业、组织以及公共系统创造稳定、真实而持续的价值。

这种价值不一定来自宏大的成果，它也可能来自一个更清晰的决策流程、一个更加可靠的人类确认环节、一套减少责任混乱的系统，或者帮助一家小企业更容易进入数字时代。

我同样关注人工智能时代的人类能力与就业转型。

人工智能会改变许多工作，但人的价值不会因此消失。判断、维护、确认、沟通、修复、协调以及现实交付仍然需要人去完成。真正重要的问题，并不是机器能够完成什么，而是社会如何帮助更多人在新的时代继续创造价值。

我的研究持续生长于美国的学习环境与现实实践，同时也希望能够为更广泛的全球社会提供参考。

复杂系统、人工智能治理、人类判断以及责任边界，并不是某一个国家独有的问题，而是未来社会共同面对的长期课题。

我始终相信，研究真正的意义，并不在于证明一个人知道多少，而在于这些知识最终能否帮助现实世界变得更加清楚、更负责任，也更加有人性。

这也是我继续学习、继续研究、继续建设的原因。

# July 14, 2026 | The Purpose of My Research: Bringing Knowledge Into the Real World

I continue to study AI governance, complex systems, human judgment, and decision architecture not to create more concepts, nor to follow short-term technological trends.

What has sustained my research is a practical question that continues to grow in importance: as technology becomes increasingly embedded in everyday life, how can people continue to understand the systems they participate in, preserve meaningful judgment, prevent responsibility from unintentionally drifting across complex processes, and ensure that technology genuinely serves humanity rather than making the world more difficult to understand?

I currently continue my learning and independent research in the United States. This environment allows me to engage with different social structures, technological practices, business settings, and real-world challenges, while continually reconsidering the relationship between people and technology.

My goal is not to remain within theoretical discussion. I aim to gradually translate long-term research into practical value that helps communities, businesses, organizations, and individuals make clearer decisions, use technology more responsibly, and preserve necessary layers of human confirmation, responsibility boundaries, and real-world judgment.

I remain especially interested in people who are often overlooked in discussions about technology.

They may be small-business owners, ordinary workers, people with limited technical experience, individuals learning new tools, or those carrying real responsibilities inside rapidly changing systems without sufficient resources or influence.

Technological progress should not benefit only those who already possess the greatest access and resources.

Technology becomes genuinely valuable when it also helps ordinary people reduce barriers to understanding, improve the way they work, discover new opportunities, and preserve dignity, choice, and agency during periods of change.

My research is primarily concerned with practical challenges facing communities and organizations in the United States, with the goal of creating stable, practical, and lasting public value through long-term work.

That value does not necessarily begin with dramatic breakthroughs. It may begin with a clearer decision process, a more reliable layer of human confirmation, a system that reduces responsibility confusion, or a small business becoming more capable of participating in the digital world.

I am equally interested in human capability and workforce transformation in the age of artificial intelligence.

AI will undoubtedly change many forms of work, but human value will not disappear. Judgment, maintenance, confirmation, communication, repair, coordination, and real-world delivery will continue to require people. The important question is not only what machines can accomplish, but how society enables more people to continue creating value in a changing world.

Although my research continues to grow through learning and practical experience in the United States, I hope it can also provide useful knowledge for broader global society.

Complex systems, AI governance, human judgment, and responsibility boundaries are not challenges limited to a single nation. They are long-term questions that increasingly belong to humanity as a whole.

I believe the true purpose of research is not measured by how much a person knows, but by whether that knowledge ultimately helps the real world become clearer, more responsible, and more humane.

That is why I continue to learn, continue to research, and continue to build.
