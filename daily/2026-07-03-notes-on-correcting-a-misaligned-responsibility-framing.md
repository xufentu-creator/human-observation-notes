# 2026-07-03 关于纠正责任表述偏移的记录

## 中文版本

今天在准备一篇新的工作论文时，我发现草稿已经偏离了这项研究长期坚持的核心原则。

这篇草稿是在 AI 辅助写作过程中逐渐形成的。表面上，它使用了我一直讨论的人类判断、责任、系统、版本、行动与后果等词语，整体看起来也很完整。但重新阅读以后，我发现其中的推理方向已经发生了变化。

草稿把人类责任描述成一种可以在版本、系统、决定、行动与后果之间持续延伸的关系。这并不代表我的研究立场。这样的表述会把人类判断从保护人的结构性边界，改变成一种可以把责任持续附着到个人身上的机制。

看起来只是几个概念之间的连接发生了变化，实际结果却完全不同。

我的研究并不是要扩大人类责任，更不是要让一个人因为曾经参与过某个阶段，就自动承担后续版本、系统变化、下游使用、他人决定或扩张后果所产生的责任。我的研究要保护的，恰恰是人类不被无理由、自动延伸或未经本人确认的责任结构持续绑定。

我在草稿发布后不久发现了这个问题，并将其删除。

删除并不意味着这件事没有发生。相反，我决定留下这份纠正记录，明确说明那篇草稿为什么被撤下、其中什么内容不代表我的思想，以及哪一条研究主线仍然有效。

那份草稿与 *Judgment Before Momentum* Version 1.0.2 所确立的核心研究方向相冲突，因此不能继续保留为当前研究成果，也不能被理解为对原有立场的修订或延伸。

我的立场没有改变：

> 判断必须先于技术动量。

人类判断是一种保护性的结构约束。它必须保护一个人在具有现实后果的行动发生之前，真实理解、质疑、拒绝、修改、暂停和纠正的能力。

一个人出现在系统中，并不自动建立其个人责任。

参与某个流程、出现在一条记录里、拥有一个正式头衔、接受默认选项、经过某个界面，或者处在下游执行位置，都不能单独构成有意义的本人确认。

只有当一个人真实获得相关信息、充分理解决定及其后果、实际拥有选择、拒绝、停止或修改的能力，并且作出明确的本人确认时，才可能讨论其个人责任。

> 没有有意义的本人确认，就没有个人责任。

这并不意味着责任消失。它意味着责任不能为了方便，被转移到离流程最近、最容易被看见的人身上。责任必须继续留在真正拥有相关信息、决定权、控制能力和行动能力的行为者、机构、系统所有者、决策主体或组织结构上。

一次参与也不能自动授权未来。

一个人曾经确认过某个版本，并不代表其自动确认了后续版本、修改后的系统、新的使用方式、扩大的影响或由其他人作出的决定。只要关键事实、系统条件、决策范围或可预见后果发生变化，就需要重新建立知情、理解、选择和本人确认。

今天的错误也让我更清楚地看见 AI 辅助写作中的一种风险：模型可以保留作者熟悉的词汇，却改变这些词之间的关系。生成的内容可能很流畅、很完整，也可能看起来与原研究非常接近，但真正被改变的，是思想的方向。

那篇草稿表达的并不是我真正想说的内容，而是 AI 根据更常见的责任逻辑自动补全出来的方向。

AI 可以辅助语言、结构和整理，但不能替作者决定最终思想。只要作者没有真实理解并明确确认最终推理，生成内容就不能自动被视为作者本人的研究立场。

> AI 生成的连贯性，不等于作者本人的确认。

我删除了错误草稿，但保留了这份记录。不是为了延续错误，而是为了明确拒绝错误。

这次纠正不修改，也不取代 *Judgment Before Momentum* Version 1.0.2。它继续作为这条研究路径的基础性规范锚点。

## 当下观察

研究记录不应该只保存最终成果，也应该保存作者发现某种表达已经偏离核心原则，并主动停止、删除和纠正它的时刻。

真正的连续性，不是让所有已经发布的内容都继续存在，而是当错误出现时，仍然能够辨认什么属于原有研究，什么已经被明确拒绝。

今天我删除了一篇偏离核心思想的草稿，但留下了纠正记录。

这份记录要说明的是：我的研究不是让人承担更多责任，而是把人从无理由、自动延伸、未经本人真实确认的责任结构中解放出来。

人类判断必须继续作为保护人的边界，而不能被重新解释成自动附加责任的通道。

# 2026-07-03 Notes on Correcting a Misaligned Responsibility Framing

## English Version

Today, while preparing a new working paper, I discovered that the draft had moved away from the governing principle of my research.

The draft had developed through AI-assisted writing. On the surface, it used terms that regularly appear in my work, including human judgment, responsibility, systems, versions, actions, and consequences. The text also appeared coherent and structurally complete. However, after reviewing it more carefully, I found that the direction of the reasoning had changed.

The draft framed human responsibility as a continuous relation extending across versions, systems, decisions, actions, and consequences. That framing did not represent my research position. It risked turning human judgment from a protective structural boundary into a mechanism through which responsibility could remain attached to a person over time.

The change appeared small at the level of wording, but the resulting position was fundamentally different.

My research does not seek to expand human responsibility. It does not hold that a person’s earlier participation automatically makes them responsible for later versions, system changes, downstream uses, decisions made by others, or expanded consequences.

The purpose of this research is to protect human beings from responsibility that arises without reason, extends automatically, or is assigned without their own meaningful confirmation.

I identified the problem shortly after the draft was published and removed it.

Removing the draft does not mean that the event did not occur. I chose to preserve this correction record so that the reason for the withdrawal, the rejected framing, and the continuing research position remain visible.

The draft conflicted with the core research line established in *Judgment Before Momentum*, Version 1.0.2. It therefore cannot remain as a current research output and must not be interpreted as a revision or extension of that foundational position.

My position remains unchanged:

> Judgment must precede technological momentum.

Human judgment functions as a protective structural constraint. It must preserve a person’s real capacity to understand, question, refuse, modify, suspend, and correct consequential action before personal responsibility is assigned.

Human presence alone does not establish personal responsibility.

Participation in a workflow, appearance in a record, possession of a formal title, acceptance of a default selection, passage through an interface, or presence in a downstream operational position does not by itself constitute meaningful personal confirmation.

Personal responsibility can only be discussed when the person had genuine access to relevant information, sufficient understanding of the decision and its consequences, real authority to choose, refuse, stop, or modify the action, and provided explicit personal confirmation.

> No meaningful personal confirmation, no personal responsibility.

This does not mean that responsibility disappears. It means that responsibility must not be transferred, for convenience, to the nearest or most visible person in a workflow. Responsibility must remain with the actor, institution, system owner, decision authority, or organizational structure that possessed the relevant knowledge, authority, control, and capacity to act.

Earlier participation does not automatically authorize the future.

A person’s confirmation of one version does not automatically confirm later versions, modified systems, new uses, expanded effects, or decisions made by others. When material facts, system conditions, decision scope, or foreseeable consequences change, renewed knowledge, understanding, choice, and personal confirmation are required.

Today’s error also clarified an important risk in AI-assisted writing: a model may preserve an author’s familiar vocabulary while changing the relationship among the concepts. The generated text may be fluent, complete, and close to the surface language of the original research, while the actual direction of the thought has shifted.

The withdrawn draft expressed not what I intended, but what the AI completed according to a more common responsibility logic.

AI may assist with language, structure, and organization, but it cannot determine the author’s final position. Unless the author has genuinely understood and explicitly confirmed the final reasoning, generated content must not automatically be treated as the author’s research position.

> AI-generated coherence is not authorial confirmation.

I removed the misaligned draft, but I preserved this correction record. The purpose is not to continue the error, but to make the rejection visible.

This correction does not revise or replace *Judgment Before Momentum*, Version 1.0.2. That work remains the foundational canonical anchor of this research line.

## Current Observation

A research record should preserve not only completed conclusions, but also the moments when an author recognizes that a framing has moved away from the governing principle and deliberately stops, removes, and corrects it.

Continuity does not require every published text to remain in place. It requires the ability to distinguish what belongs to the established research line from what has been explicitly rejected.

Today, I removed a draft that had moved away from my core position, but I preserved a record of the correction.

The purpose of this record is to make one point clear: my research does not seek to make human beings bear more responsibility. It seeks to free them from responsibility that arises without reason, extends automatically, or is assigned without their own meaningful confirmation.

Human judgment must remain a protective boundary. It must not be reinterpreted as a channel through which responsibility is automatically attached, extended, or transferred.
