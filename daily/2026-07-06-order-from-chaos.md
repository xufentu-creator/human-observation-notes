2026-07-06｜从混沌中寻找次序


人类好像一直是在混沌中生长出来的。

以前我理解“复杂系统”，更多是把它当成一个研究词。后来慢慢发现，它其实不是离生活很远的概念。一个人每天遇到的信息、关系、选择、误会、责任、机会和风险，本身就是一个复杂系统。很多时候，问题并不是没有信息，而是信息太多以后，人反而不知道该相信什么、该停在哪里、该由谁确认、又该往哪里走。

我现在才慢慢明白，人类寻找次序的过程，可能从来不是先有完整答案，再去执行。更多时候，是人在混乱里反复碰撞，慢慢看见某些规律，然后把这些规律变成语言、规则、工具、制度和共同生活的方式。

这也让我重新理解一些古老的神话。比如盘古开天辟地，它不一定只是一个关于宇宙起源的故事，也可以被看作人类对“从混沌到秩序”的一种早期想象。混沌不是空无，而是一切还没有被分开、还没有被命名、还没有被整理成结构的状态。所谓开天辟地，也许就是一种最早的判断：哪里需要分开，哪里需要连接，哪里需要边界，哪里需要方向。

人的智慧也许从来不是孤立存在的，而是在环境里被逼出来的。一个时代有一个时代的混乱，一个时代也会长出一种管理方式。部落、家族、国家、市场、公司、平台、算法和 AI，每一种新的组织方式或技术形态出现，都会把原来的次序重新打乱，也会逼迫人类重新学习如何判断。

所以我现在越来越觉得，真正重要的不是单纯拥有更多技术，而是在技术越来越快的时候，人还能不能保留判断的位置。AI 可以帮助整理信息，可以加快生成，可以扩大连接，但当结果影响真实的人、真实的责任和真实的生活时，人类判断仍然不能消失。

这也是我为什么会继续写复杂系统、AI Governance、Human Judgment、Decision Architecture 和 Responsibility Drift。它们不是我突然想出来的一组词，而是我在现实生活、技术变化和人类关系里反复看到的问题：当系统越来越复杂，责任很容易漂移；当信息越来越多，判断反而更容易模糊；当工具越来越强，人更需要知道自己在什么地方必须停下来确认。

很多时候，新的东西并不是因为世界已经清楚了才出现，而是因为现实中已经出现了某种混乱。人需要记录，需要确认，需要追溯，需要在看不见的过程里保留一些可被理解的痕迹。真正有价值的东西，不只是看起来多聪明，而是能不能帮助人在复杂环境里保留清醒、边界和责任。

我现在理解的“次序”，不是把所有东西都控制住，也不是把世界变成一个没有变化的系统。真正的次序，可能是在变化中还能找到判断的位置，在混乱中还能留下可以被理解的记录，在快速行动之前还能问一句：这件事由谁确认？责任停在哪里？人有没有真正理解？

从混沌中寻找次序，并不意味着人类可以彻底消除混沌。混沌可能一直存在，只是每一代人都会在自己的时代里，找到一种暂时能够生活、判断和合作的方式。后来新的技术出现，旧的次序又会被打乱，人类又重新开始整理。

这也许就是人类一直在做的事情。

不是一次性找到永远正确的答案，而是在混乱中不断形成新的边界，在不确定中重新建立判断，在变化中保存一些能够被后来人继续理解的东西。

当下观察

人类不是从完整规则里长出来的，而是在混沌中不断寻找规则。

每一次新的工具、制度或技术形态出现，都不只是能力扩张，也是一种新的次序尝试。

AI 时代真正需要保存的，不只是速度，而是人在复杂系统中的判断位置。

July 6, 2026｜Finding Order Within Chaos


Human beings seem to have grown out of chaos.

In the past, I understood “complex systems” mostly as a research term. Later, I slowly realized that it is not something far away from daily life. The information, relationships, choices, misunderstandings, responsibilities, opportunities, and risks that a person faces every day already form a complex system. Many times, the problem is not that there is no information. The problem is that once there is too much information, people no longer know what to trust, where to stop, who should confirm, or which direction to take.

I am only beginning to understand that the human search for order may never have started with a complete answer. More often, people collide with uncertainty, gradually notice patterns, and then turn those patterns into language, rules, tools, institutions, and ways of living together.

This also makes me understand ancient myths differently. The story of Pangu separating heaven and earth does not have to be seen only as a story about the origin of the universe. It can also be understood as an early human imagination of moving from chaos toward order. Chaos is not emptiness. It is a state in which things have not yet been separated, named, or organized into structure. To separate heaven and earth may also be an early act of judgment: where something needs to be divided, where something needs to be connected, where a boundary is needed, and where direction begins.

Human wisdom may never exist in isolation. It is often forced out by the conditions people live in. Every era has its own form of confusion, and every era grows its own form of management. Tribes, families, states, markets, companies, platforms, algorithms, and AI all reshape order in different ways. Every new organizational form or technological structure disrupts an earlier order and forces human beings to relearn how to judge.

This is why I increasingly feel that the most important thing is not simply having more technology, but whether human beings can still preserve the position of judgment when technology becomes faster. AI can help organize information, accelerate generation, and expand connection. But when the result affects real people, real responsibility, and real life, human judgment cannot disappear.

This is also why I continue to write about complex systems, AI Governance, Human Judgment, Decision Architecture, and Responsibility Drift. These are not just words I suddenly chose. They come from repeated observations in real life, technological change, and human relationships: when systems become more complex, responsibility can easily drift; when information increases, judgment can become more unclear; when tools become more powerful, people need to know even more clearly where human confirmation must remain.

Often, new things do not appear because the world has already become clear. They appear because a certain form of disorder has already emerged in reality. People need records, confirmation, traceability, and ways to preserve understandable traces within processes that are otherwise difficult to see. What truly matters is not simply whether something appears intelligent, but whether it can help people preserve clarity, boundaries, and responsibility within complex environments.

The order I now understand is not about controlling everything, nor is it about turning the world into a system without change. Real order may mean finding the position of judgment within change, leaving understandable records within confusion, and asking before action becomes too fast: Who confirmed this? Where does responsibility stop? Did the human being truly understand?

Finding order within chaos does not mean that human beings can completely eliminate chaos. Chaos may always remain. Each generation simply finds, within its own era, a temporary way to live, judge, and cooperate. Later, new technologies appear, old orders are disrupted again, and human beings begin the work of reorganization once more.

Perhaps this is what human beings have always been doing.

Not finding one permanently correct answer, but forming new boundaries within confusion, rebuilding judgment within uncertainty, and preserving something that later generations can continue to understand.

Current Observation

Human beings did not grow out of complete rules. They have been searching for rules within chaos.

Every new tool, institution, or technological form is not only an expansion of capability, but also an attempt to form a new order.

In the AI era, what must be preserved is not only speed, but the position of human judgment within complex systems.
