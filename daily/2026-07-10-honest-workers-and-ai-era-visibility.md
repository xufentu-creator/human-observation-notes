2026-07-06｜诚实干活的人，也应该被 AI 时代看见

今天我想到一种很普通、也很容易被忽略的人。

他们不是特别会说话，也不会包装自己，不懂复杂的数字工具，不会写漂亮的介绍，不会做网页，不会在平台上展示自己，更不会用 AI 把自己的能力表达得很好。可是他们很诚实，愿意干活，能把手上的事情做完，也不会想着骗人。

在过去的社会里，这样的人也许还能靠口碑、熟人、手艺和长期劳动慢慢生活。一个人只要踏实、可靠、愿意吃苦，总还是能被周围的人看见。可是到了 AI 时代，社会变化太快了，很多机会已经不只是给“会做事的人”，而是给“会表达、会被搜索、会被系统识别、会进入平台规则的人”。

这对很多只会认真干活的人来说，其实很残酷。

他们不是没有价值，也不是不努力。只是他们没有跟上新的表达方式，没有把自己的能力翻译成数字系统能够理解的语言。系统看不见他们，平台推荐不到他们，客户搜索不到他们，AI 也不知道该怎样把他们介绍出来。最后，一个真实可靠的人，可能输给一个更会包装的人。

这不是他们一个人的失败，也不应该只被解释成“不学习就被淘汰”。社会发展太快的时候，总会有一部分人被推到边缘。尤其是那些年纪大一点、教育资源少一点、语言能力弱一点、长期靠体力和手艺生活的人，他们不是不想适应，而是不知道从哪里开始。

他们也是人。

一个只会干活的人，可能不懂系统，不懂营销，不懂 AI，但他可能比很多会说话的人更守信用。一个不会写介绍的人，可能几十年都在认真做同一种工作。一个不会上传资料的人，可能真的有经验、有责任心，也知道怎样把事情做好。社会如果只奖励表达能力，而看不见真实劳动，就是另一种不公平。

AI 时代不应该只让强者更强，也应该帮助那些还没有被系统看见的人重新被看见。

技术真正有意义的地方，不只是替聪明的人提高效率，也应该帮助普通人降低进入新系统的门槛。让不会写的人也能把自己的能力说清楚，让不会整理的人也能保存自己的资料，让不会上平台的人也能被客户找到，让诚实干活的人不因为不会包装自己就被时代甩开。

我越来越觉得，未来的工具、平台和制度都应该多考虑这种人。不是每个人都要变成创业者、演说家、内容创作者或数字专家。社会仍然需要安装的人、维修的人、照顾人的人、搬东西的人、做饭的人、清洁的人、种地的人、开车的人、手工劳动的人。真正的世界不是只靠屏幕运行的。

如果 AI 时代把语言、展示、搜索、排名和自动推荐都变成新的入口，那么我们就更需要保护那些不会轻易进入这些入口的人。否则，技术越先进，真实劳动反而越容易被隐藏。

这也让我重新理解“人类判断”的意义。判断不只是判断系统是否正确，也包括判断一个人是否被系统公平地看见。一个人的价值不能只由页面、关键词、推荐算法或数字资料决定。很多人的可靠性，是长期生活、劳动、关系和责任中形成的，不一定能被系统马上识别。

社会应该给这些人一些支持和帮助。

不是施舍，而是承认他们本来就有价值。帮助他们学习新的工具，帮助他们整理资料，帮助他们建立基本的数字身份，帮助他们把真实能力说清楚，也帮助社会不要把“不会表达”误判成“没有能力”。

AI 时代真正好的发展，不应该是把一部分人变得更快，然后让另一部分人消失。真正好的发展，应该是在系统变快的时候，仍然给普通人留下进入的门。

---
当下观察

一个诚实、能干活、但不懂数字系统的人，在 AI 时代很容易变得可怜。

不是因为他没有价值，而是因为新的系统不一定看得见他的价值。

社会发展越快，越需要有人帮助这些普通人被看见、被理解、被连接。

AI 时代不应该只奖励会表达的人，也应该保护那些真实劳动、诚实生活、却不会包装自己的人。

July 6, 2026｜Honest Workers Should Also Be Seen in the AI Era


Today I thought about a very ordinary kind of person, one who is also very easy to overlook.

They may not be good at speaking. They may not know how to present themselves. They may not understand complex digital tools. They may not know how to write a polished introduction, build a website, display themselves on platforms, or use AI to describe their abilities clearly. But they are honest. They are willing to work. They can finish the task in front of them, and they are not trying to deceive others.

In the past, people like this could still survive through reputation, acquaintances, craftsmanship, and long-term labor. If a person was reliable, practical, and willing to work hard, they could still be seen by the people around them. But in the AI era, society is changing too quickly. Many opportunities are no longer given only to those who can do the work. They are increasingly given to those who can express themselves, be searched, be recognized by systems, and enter platform rules.

For many people who only know how to work honestly, this is harsh.

They are not without value. They are not unwilling to work hard. They simply have not kept up with the new forms of expression. They have not translated their abilities into language that digital systems can understand. Systems cannot see them. Platforms do not recommend them. Customers cannot find them. AI does not know how to introduce them. In the end, a genuinely reliable person may lose to someone who is simply better at presentation.

This is not only the failure of the individual. It should not be reduced to a simple statement that those who do not learn deserve to be eliminated. When society develops too quickly, some people will always be pushed to the edge. Especially older workers, people with fewer educational resources, people with weaker language skills, and people who have lived for years through physical work and practical skill. It is not always that they refuse to adapt. Often, they simply do not know where to begin.

They are human beings too.

A person who only knows how to work may not understand systems, marketing, or AI, but they may be more trustworthy than many people who speak well. A person who cannot write an introduction may have spent decades doing the same kind of work carefully. A person who cannot upload documents may still have real experience, responsibility, and the ability to do things well. If society only rewards expression and fails to see real labor, that is another form of unfairness.

The AI era should not only make the strong stronger. It should also help those who have not yet been seen by systems become visible again.

The real meaning of technology should not be only to improve efficiency for people who are already capable. It should also lower the threshold for ordinary people to enter new systems. It should help those who cannot write explain their abilities clearly, help those who cannot organize preserve their materials, help those who cannot use platforms be found by customers, and help honest workers avoid being left behind simply because they do not know how to present themselves.

I increasingly feel that future tools, platforms, and institutions should take these people into account. Not everyone needs to become an entrepreneur, speaker, content creator, or digital expert. Society still needs people who install, repair, care for others, carry things, cook, clean, farm, drive, and do manual work. The real world does not run only through screens.

If language, display, search, ranking, and automated recommendation become new gateways in the AI era, then we need even more protection for those who do not easily enter these gateways. Otherwise, the more advanced technology becomes, the easier it may be for real labor to be hidden.

This also makes me understand human judgment differently. Judgment is not only about whether a system is correct. It is also about whether a person is being fairly seen by the system. A person’s value cannot be determined only by a page, keywords, recommendation algorithms, or digital records. Many people’s reliability is formed through long-term life, labor, relationships, and responsibility. It may not be immediately visible to a system.

Society should give these people support and help.

Not as charity, but as recognition that they already have value. Help them learn new tools. Help them organize materials. Help them build a basic digital identity. Help them explain their real abilities clearly. And help society avoid mistaking “not knowing how to express” for “having no ability.”

Good development in the AI era should not mean making some people faster while allowing others to disappear. Good development should mean that when systems become faster, ordinary people still have a door through which they can enter.

Current Observation

An honest person who can work but does not understand digital systems can easily become vulnerable in the AI era.

Not because they have no value, but because new systems may not be able to see their value.

The faster society develops, the more it needs ways to help ordinary people be seen, understood, and connected.

The AI era should not only reward those who can express themselves. It should also protect those who work honestly, live sincerely, and do not know how to package themselves.
