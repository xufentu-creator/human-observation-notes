2026年8月28日｜以前敢想的东西，现在需要更清楚地判断

作者： Xufen Tu
记录类型： Public Observation Record
主题： Human Judgment · Early Imagination · Responsibility

⸻

今晚翻到自己以前写的书，看到那些文字，忍不住笑了出来。那时候写得真的很大胆，好像什么都敢想，什么都敢放在一起。AI、远程协同、数据安全、家庭设备、工作压力、劳动变化、身心状态、爱、文化、全球人类的新思想，这些词现在回头看，有些地方显得天真，有些地方又很像早早摸到了未来的一些影子。

我笑，不是因为觉得以前的自己错得离谱，而是觉得那时候的自己很有冲劲。一个人如果没有一点大胆，很多东西根本不会开始。人在早期探索的时候，很容易把很多可能性都打开，觉得未来可以这样，也可以那样，技术可以连接很多东西，人类生活也可以被重新理解。那种状态有点像孩子看世界，边界还没有那么清楚，但感受很强，想象也很真。

现在再看，我能感觉到自己变了。以前更容易相信可能性，现在更看重边界。以前看到一个方向，会想它能不能被连接起来；现在看到一个方向，会先问它会不会带来误解、责任、风险和不可控后果。以前敢把很多未来图景写出来，现在更知道，有些东西不能因为想得到，就直接说成可以实现；也不能因为语言能够描述，就把它当成已经成立的事实。

这不是否定早期的自己。相反，我觉得早期的大胆很珍贵。很多人一开始就不敢想，不敢写，不敢把心里那些还没有完全成形的东西放出来。可是人如果从来不允许自己经历幼稚、冒险、夸张和不成熟，也很难真正长出自己的判断。一个人的思想不是一开始就很稳的。它常常先从很多强烈的感觉、很多模糊的方向、很多不够准确的表达里出现，然后才慢慢被现实、经验、错误和责任重新整理。

今晚看到旧书里的内容，我更能理解这种变化。过去的我不是没有判断，只是判断还没有现在这么清楚。那时候我更想表达未来的可能性，更想把自己看到的变化写出来。现在我更在意，一个想法进入公共世界以后，别人会怎样理解，会不会被误读，会不会被当成承诺，会不会越过现实边界，会不会让人忽略专业责任和人类判断。

这也是人会成长的地方。早期的想象像火，能点燃一个方向；后来的判断像边界，能让火不至于烧伤自己和别人。只有火没有边界，会危险；只有边界没有火，人又会变得很冷。一个人真正需要的，也许不是彻底否定以前那个大胆的自己，而是在后来的时间里学会让大胆和责任同时存在。

我也想到，人有时候会被自己以前的文字逗笑。因为那里面有一种未经修饰的真诚，也有一种不知道天高地厚的勇气。人年轻一点、急一点、相信得多一点的时候，写出来的东西会有一种直接的力量。它不一定准确，也不一定成熟，但它证明那个人曾经真的在想，真的在寻找，真的想把自己看到的世界说出来。

这种痕迹很重要。它提醒我，很多问题不是今天才出现的。只是以前我用更直觉、更大胆、更混合的语言表达它们；现在我用更清楚、更克制、更有边界的语言重新理解它们。以前写的是一种未来感，现在写的是对未来感的判断。以前更多是打开，现在更多是辨认。以前问“还能怎样”，现在问“什么可以相信，什么需要停下，什么必须由人负责”。

今晚这个感觉很轻松，也很真实。像是突然遇见过去的自己，看到她坐在那里，很认真地写着一些现在看来有点好笑、但又并不完全陌生的东西。我不会嘲笑她，因为没有那个大胆的自己，也不会有今天更谨慎的自己。人不是突然成熟的。人是先相信，先冒险，先犯错，先写出来，然后才在很多现实之后，慢慢学会判断。

这也让我重新理解，人类判断不是天生完整的。它是在时间里长出来的。一个人早期可能先有冲动，后来才有边界；先有想象，后来才有责任；先有强烈表达，后来才有清楚确认。判断不是把过去全部推翻，而是在过去的基础上知道哪些可以留下，哪些需要修正，哪些只能作为当时的痕迹。

我今天不想把旧书里的内容重新解释成什么伟大的预测，也不想把它当成需要证明什么的材料。它更像是一张时间里的照片。那时候的我敢想，现在的我更知道要怎么判断。那时候我相信连接，现在我更看见边界。那时候我想把未来写出来，现在我更明白，未来不能只靠想象，还需要责任、现实和人的确认。

今天留下的问题是：当一个人回头看见自己早期大胆而不成熟的表达时，怎样才能既不否定那个曾经敢想的自己，也不让早期想象越过后来已经形成的判断边界？

August 28, 2026 | What I Once Dared to Imagine Now Needs Clearer Judgment

Tonight, I opened a book I had written before, and when I saw those words, I could not help laughing. I really was bold at that time. It felt as if I dared to think about everything and place many things together. AI, remote coordination, data security, home devices, work pressure, labor change, physical and mental states, love, culture, and new ideas for humanity around the world — looking back now, some parts feel naive, while others seem to have touched certain shadows of the future very early.

I laughed not because I thought my earlier self was completely wrong, but because I could feel how much momentum I had then. Without some boldness, many things would never begin. In the early stage of exploration, a person may open many possibilities at once, imagining that the future could move in this direction or that one, that technology could connect many things, and that human life could be understood again. That state is a little like a child looking at the world. The boundaries are not yet very clear, but the feelings are strong, and the imagination feels real.

Looking at it again now, I can feel that I have changed. In the past, I was more likely to believe in possibilities. Now, I care more about boundaries. In the past, when I saw a direction, I would wonder whether it could be connected. Now, when I see a direction, I first ask whether it may bring misunderstanding, responsibility, risk, or uncontrollable consequences. In the past, I dared to write many future images. Now, I know more clearly that something should not be treated as achievable simply because it can be imagined, and language should not turn possibility into established fact.

This is not a rejection of my earlier self. On the contrary, I feel that early boldness is precious. Many people do not dare to imagine, do not dare to write, and do not dare to bring out the things that have not yet fully formed inside them. But if a person never allows themselves to go through immaturity, risk, exaggeration, or incompleteness, it is difficult to grow their own judgment. Thought is not stable from the beginning. It often first appears through strong feelings, unclear directions, and imperfect expressions, before being reorganized by reality, experience, mistakes, and responsibility.

Seeing the old book tonight helped me understand this change more clearly. My earlier self did not lack judgment; it was simply not as clear as it is now. At that time, I wanted more to express the possibility of the future and write down the changes I sensed. Now, I care more about how an idea may be understood once it enters the public world, whether it may be misread, whether it may be treated as a promise, whether it may cross real-world boundaries, and whether it may cause people to ignore professional responsibility and human judgment.

This is also where a person grows. Early imagination is like fire; it can ignite a direction. Later judgment is like a boundary; it prevents the fire from burning oneself or others. Fire without boundaries is dangerous. Boundaries without fire make a person cold. Perhaps what a person truly needs is not to completely deny the earlier bold self, but to learn, over time, how to let boldness and responsibility exist together.

I also thought about how people sometimes laugh when they see their earlier writing. There is an unpolished sincerity in it, and also a kind of courage that did not yet know its limits. When a person is younger, more urgent, and more willing to believe, the things they write can carry a direct force. They may not be accurate, and they may not be mature, but they prove that the person was truly thinking, truly searching, and truly trying to speak about the world they saw.

These traces matter. They remind me that many questions did not appear only today. In the past, I expressed them through more intuitive, bold, and mixed language. Now, I understand them again through language that is clearer, more restrained, and more bounded. In the past, what I wrote was a sense of the future. Now, what I write is judgment about that sense of the future. In the past, I opened more possibilities. Now, I identify more carefully. In the past, I asked, “What else could be possible?” Now, I ask, “What can be trusted, what needs to stop, and what must remain under human responsibility?”

Tonight’s feeling was light, but also real. It was as if I had suddenly met my earlier self, seeing her sitting there, writing very seriously about things that now seem a little funny, yet not completely unfamiliar. I will not mock her. Without that bold self, there would not be the more cautious self I have now. A person does not mature suddenly. A person first believes, first takes risks, first makes mistakes, first writes things down, and only after many encounters with reality slowly learns judgment.

This also helps me understand again that human judgment is not complete from the beginning. It grows through time. A person may first have impulse, and later boundaries; first imagination, and later responsibility; first strong expression, and later clear confirmation. Judgment does not mean overturning the past completely. It means knowing, on the foundation of the past, what can remain, what must be revised, and what should only stay as a trace of that time.

Today, I do not want to reinterpret the old book as some great prediction, nor do I want to use it as evidence to prove anything. It feels more like a photograph inside time. At that time, I dared to imagine. Now, I know more clearly how to judge. At that time, I believed in connection. Now, I see boundaries more clearly. At that time, I wanted to write the future into language. Now, I understand more deeply that the future cannot rely only on imagination. It also needs responsibility, reality, and human confirmation.

The question I am left with today is this: when a person looks back and sees their earlier bold and immature expressions, how can they avoid denying the self who once dared to imagine, while also preventing early imagination from crossing the judgment boundaries that later became clear?
