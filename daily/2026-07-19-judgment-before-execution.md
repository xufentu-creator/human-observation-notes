# 2026年7月19日
# 判断在前，执行在后

今天与一位研究人工智能的朋友交流了我的论文和研究方向。

交流结束后，我再次确认了一个已经持续思考很长时间的问题。

随着人工智能越来越多地参与现实世界，真正值得研究的，不只是模型是否更准确，也不仅是系统是否更高效，而是人类判断在整个系统中的位置。

我越来越倾向于一个研究立场：

> **人类判断应位于具有现实后果的系统执行之前。**

很多讨论关注的是如何让系统更快、更智能、更自动化。

但现实世界里的执行，往往意味着真实的影响已经发生。

一次付款、一项审批、一次身份标记、一条自动指令，都可能在执行之后产生无法简单撤回的现实结果。

因此，我关注的问题并不是是否使用人工智能，而是在人与系统共同参与决策时，人类判断应当处于什么位置。

我认为，这是一个值得持续研究的问题。

今天的交流也让我再次意识到，真正需要长期保持一致的，不是不断改变表达，而是持续围绕同一个核心问题进行观察、记录和研究。

未来，我仍会继续围绕这一主题进行公开研究，并通过论文、研究日记和公开记录不断完善自己的思考。

这篇日记只是记录今天再次确认的一点：

> **判断在前，执行在后。**

这是我当前公开研究持续发展的核心立场之一。

# July 19, 2026
# Judgment Before Execution

Today I discussed my research and published papers with a friend who also studies artificial intelligence.

After our conversation, I found myself returning to a question that has remained central throughout my work.

As artificial intelligence increasingly participates in real-world activities, an important question is not only how capable AI becomes, but where human judgment should remain within systems that produce real-world consequences.

My current research position can be summarized as follows:

> **Human judgment should precede consequential system execution.**

Many discussions focus on making systems faster, more capable, or more autonomous.

However, real-world execution often means that real consequences have already occurred.

A payment, an approval, an identity classification, or an automated instruction may all produce effects that cannot simply be undone afterward.

For this reason, my research is less concerned with whether AI should be used, and more concerned with where human judgment should remain when people and AI participate together in consequential decisions.

I believe this is a question worth continuing to study.

Today's conversation also reminded me that long-term research is strengthened not by constantly changing its message, but by continuing to observe, document, and develop the same central question over time.

This diary records one point that I confirmed again today:

> **Judgment before execution.**

It represents one of the continuing public research positions that my work is exploring.
